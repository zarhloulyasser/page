The Game

Most situations that look like problems are actually games: there are other players, the payoffs depend on what everyone does, and the rules are far less fixed than they advertise. Naming which game you're in is usually more than half the work — and it's the step almost everyone skips, because the game arrives pre-packaged in a frame you inherited for free.

Three questions

Before playing, these are worth answering honestly:

  1. Who are the players, really? Not who's in the room — who is affected by the outcome and can act on it. Those two sets almost never match.
  2. What's actually being scored? The stated objective and the rewarded behaviour come apart almost immediately in any system that runs long enough.
  3. Is the game finite or infinite? Finite games are played to win. Infinite games are played to keep playing. Treating one as the other is the most expensive category error available.

Changing the game

The highest-leverage move is usually not a better move inside the game. It's a move on the game itself — changing who is playing, what is scored, or when it ends. This is nearly always available and nearly never attempted, because the rules feel like physics right up until someone demonstrates that they aren't.
