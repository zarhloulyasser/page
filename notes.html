Dr K explains his side of the Dr Mike debate

Provisional — distilled without full transcript, from the debate and summaries. Video · Original debate (Ayurveda)

The disagreement: Dr Mike holds population-level RCTs as the standard, and argues Ayurveda without that evidence breeds misunderstanding, hucksters, and diverts attention from improving medicine. Dr K says he uses evidence-based techniques with most patients, and uses complementary / Ayurvedic approaches where patients can't wait for science — including evidence-proven parts as complementary, and individualized (N-of-1) treatment where population RCTs fit poorly.

Frames

  • Two sampling functions: population RCT lens vs clinician N-of-1 lens. Each makes different evidence salient.
  • Burden of proof asymmetry: disagreeing with consensus bodies requires unusually strong evidence — or evidence of corruption / why the consensus misses something.
  • Mechanism vs effect: a practice (e.g. breath / prana / cardiac coherence) can show effects before science has a mechanism.
  • Expert opinion is the lowest form of evidence and should update with research.
  • Absence of evidence-based voices online leaves room for misinformation to flourish.

Questions this raises for me

  • Which truths here can only be evaluated by doing — by training a capacity — rather than by reading trials?
  • What evidence would change my mind in each direction, and who bears the burden?
  • What stays true across observers: the RCT reader, the clinician, the patient who can't wait?
  • Am I exploiting a model (wait for consensus) where one sufficiently different experience would change it — or exploiting novelty where consensus is the correction?
