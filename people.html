What I take from him is less any particular position than a method: start from the assumption that the consensus view is a social artifact rather than a finding, then ask what follows if it's wrong. The famous question — what important truth do very few people agree with you on — is really a test of whether you hold any independent frames at all, or only inherited ones you've never had cause to inspect.

The part I keep arguing with: the same move that turns up a genuinely neglected truth also reliably manufactures confident nonsense, and from the inside those two feel identical. I don't think there's a clean test.
